## 1. Kim, Seok et al. — "Empirical Analysis on the Potential Causes of the Korea Discount"
**Source:** Korean Securities Association Journal, Vol. 54, No. 4 (2025)

**Original title:** 코리아 디스카운트의 잠재적 원인들에 대한 실증분석

**Abstract (translated from Korean):**
This study evaluates the explanatory power of factors commonly cited as causes of the Korea discount — such as shareholder rights and shareholder return ratios — using broader and more precise data than prior research. Analyzing data from 58 countries over 2001–2018, the study finds that Korea does not, on average, have lower shareholder rights scores than other countries, undermining the premise that this factor causes the Korea discount. Instead, high uncertainty surrounding economic policy is raised as a likely cause. The claim that low shareholder return ratios cause the Korea discount also lacks empirical support: Korean firms' shareholder return volumes were not smaller than those of firms in other markets on average, and the markets with the largest long-term increases in shareholder return ratios actually saw declines in average firm value. Meanwhile, although Korea's trading turnover ratio ranked 3rd among 58 countries, the degree to which corporate fundamentals such as profitability and growth were reflected in firm value ranked near the bottom at 51st. The predictive power of M/B ratios for future corporate growth was also significantly lower in Korea than in other markets. These findings suggest that Korea's high short-term investment culture may prevent long-term corporate growth from being fully reflected in stock prices, resulting in lower firm valuations.

**Keywords:** Korea discount, firm value, shareholder rights, shareholder returns, short-term investment tendency, value relevance, fundamentals

## 2. Lee, Jeong — "Analysis of the Korea Discount from a Corporate Capital Allocation Perspective: Focusing on Determinants of PBR Changes"
**Source:** Analysis of the Korean Economy, Vol. 31, No. 3 (2025)

**Original title:** 기업의 자본배분관점에서 코리아 디스카운트 분석: PBR 변화의 결정요인을 중심으로

**Abstract (translated from Korean):**
This study analyzes changes in the price-to-book ratio (PBR) — the key indicator of the recent value-up policy aimed at resolving the Korea discount — from the perspective of corporate capital allocation policy. Unlike prior research, both short-term and long-term changes in PBR are modeled to directly analyze the impact of cash retention, dividends, and investment policies on firm value. The results show that across all firms, increased cash retention, expanded R&D investment, increased general investment, and increased dividends all have positive long-term effects on PBR improvement. However, for large manufacturing firms, dividend expansion had a weak or even negative effect on PBR, while R&D investment was found to be more significant. This is consistent with the declining PBR trend observed alongside dividend increases in the overall KOSPI index time series. Governance improvement also had positive medium-to-long-term effects for general firms, but its effects were negligible or negative for large manufacturers, confirming differentiation between firm groups. The study contributes academically by modeling PBR changes from both short-term and long-term perspectives to clarify the impact of corporate capital allocation strategies, and particularly by deriving distinctions between large manufacturers and other firm groups, which are central to the Korean economy.

**Keywords:** Korea discount, PBR changes, changes in corporate fund allocation, large manufacturers

## 3. Lee, Jin-hyo — "A Study on the Effectiveness of the Corporate Value-Up Program"
**Source:** Law Review, Vol. 37, No. 3 (2025)

**Original title:** 기업 밸류업 프로그램의 실효성에 관한 연구

**Abstract (translated from Korean; no formal abstract — summary from introduction):**
Amid growing domestic and international economic uncertainty and prolonged domestic political uncertainty, concerns about the Korea discount (undervaluation of the domestic stock market) are intensifying. Various government-wide policies have been pursued to resolve the undervaluation and increase corporate value — including strengthening minority shareholder rights, improving global alignment and expanding the capital market, enhancing corporate governance, increasing accounting transparency, and promoting ESG management. However, the undervaluation persists due to factors including misalignment of interests between controlling and minority shareholders, declining profitability of listed companies, lack of a long-term investment base, increasing zombie firms, and lagging corporate governance. In 2024, a corporate value-up program modeled after Japan was introduced, but most KOSPI and KOSDAQ listed companies have ignored it, and the stock market closed weak that same year — the opposite result from Japan's experience. This paper examines the main contents of Korea's corporate value-up program, draws lessons from Japan's case, and proposes measures to enhance the program's effectiveness.

**Keywords:** Korea discount, corporate value-up program, value-up, corporate governance reform

## 4. Njoku, Enyeribe et al. — "Corporate Governance and Dividend Policy Under Concentrated Ownership: Evidence from Post-Reform Korea"
**Source:** Journal of Risk and Financial Management, Vol. 19, No. 2 (2026)

**Abstract (original English):**
This study investigates how ownership structure conditions the transmission of corporate governance mechanisms into dividend policy within the context of South Korea's evolving regulatory environment. Using a balanced panel of 5,022 firm-year observations from 558 non-financial KOSPI-listed firms over 2011–2019, we analyze governance quality using data from the Korea Corporate Governance Service. We employ both an aggregate score and four constituent dimensions: board effectiveness, shareholder rights protection, audit committee competency, and disclosure transparency. The empirical framework combines firm fixed effects estimation, binary logistic regressions, and a two-step dynamic System GMM approach to account for unobserved heterogeneity, payout persistence, and endogeneity. The results reveal systematic heterogeneity across ownership regimes. Among non-Chaebol firms, higher governance quality across all dimensions is associated with higher dividend payouts, consistent with the governance outcome hypothesis. In contrast, among Chaebol-affiliated firms, the effectiveness of governance mechanisms is selective rather than uniform. While the aggregate governance score and shareholder rights protection retain explanatory power for dividend outcomes, internal oversight mechanisms related to board structure, audit competency, and disclosure do not exert independent influences once ownership structure is taken into account. These findings show that concentrated ownership structures condition which governance mechanisms remain effective in shaping payout policy. Regulators seeking to mitigate valuation discounts in conglomerate-dominated economies should prioritize the substantive empowerment of minority shareholder rights, as these mechanisms retain influence over payout policy even under concentrated ownership structures.

**Keywords:** corporate governance; dividend policy; Korea discount; ownership heterogeneity; shareholder rights; substitution hypothesis

## 5. Yun, Yongsuk & Kim, Hyun Ah — "High Investor Sentiment and Initial Engagement Discounts: Evidence from Korea"
**Source:** Applied Economics, Vol. 54, No. 8 (2022)

**Abstract (original English):**
This study examines whether high sentiment affects auditors' risk assessment in a less litigious environment. Specifically, we use a Korean setting with relatively low litigation risks and investigate whether audit fees are lower when firms change auditors during high sentiment times. Lower audit fees responding to high sentiment in a weak legal environment may indicate that auditors perceive the overall audit risk including reputation risk and regulation risk as low within the given period, increasing the possibility of their failure to detect managerial opportunistic behaviour. Using 17,469 Korean public firm-year observations from 2003 to 2017, we find that initial audit fees are significantly lower when investor sentiment is high, while changing auditors has no influence on audit fees when investor sentiment is low. Furthermore, this result is maintained even when auditors are changed to Big4 or industry specialist auditors (SPEC), which are known to have high ability and risk sensitivity. We also find that Big4 and SPEC initial auditors input significantly less audit hours during high investor sentiment periods, suggesting that optimistic bias caused by high sentiment makes auditors expect that relaxed audits are tolerable regardless of the legal environment.

**Keywords:** High investor sentiment; initial engagement discounts; audit fees; audit hours; auditor change type

## 6. Choi, Tae H. et al. — "The Equity Premium Puzzle: Empirical Evidence for the 'Korea Discount'"
**Source:** Asia-Pacific Journal of Accounting & Economics, Vol. 19, No. 2 (2012)

**Abstract (original English):**
This study examines the cost of equity capital (COC) and the factors that influence the COC of listed Korean companies, and compares the COC of Korean companies with that of companies from 31 selected countries. The major research question is whether companies that are listed in an emerging market have a disadvantage as they are underpriced through a higher COC (as compared with companies that are traded in developed markets). Consistent with the "Korea Discount," we find that the COC is significantly higher for Korean companies than for companies in other countries after controlling for other relevant factors. However, the "Korea Discount" has significantly eased in recent years.

**Keywords:** analysts' forecasts; cost of capital; firm characteristics; Korea Discount; Korean stock market

## 8. Jung, Taejin et al. — "Accounting for the Korea Discount with Book-to-Market Ratios above One"
**Source:** Korean Accounting Journal, Vol. 31, No. 6 (2022)

**Abstract (original English):**
The historical average book-to-market ratio (BTM) of Korean listed companies is above one, and often used as evidence for the Korea discount. This paper asks whether the BTM reflects either risk inherent in Korean businesses as claimed in the popular press, or a bias in market expectations. Analyzing data of Korean listed firms from 1995 to 2019, we first confirm that stocks with BTM above one consistently outperform the rest, consistent with the empirical findings documented in prior studies. When we compose three summary indicators that are allegedly associated with the Korea discount (i.e., fundamental factor, geopolitical factor, and governance factor), we find that only governance factor is positively related to BTM as alluded to in the business press. In our subsequent analyses borrowed from Ohlson and Bilinski's (2015) specification, we find that BTM above one is associated with a higher probability of positive extreme returns, but a lower probability of negative extreme returns. The finding indicates that BTM above one is more likely to reflect characteristics associated with stock mispricing. We further explore why such systematic bias persists over time and report some evidence that governance factor plays a crucial role. This paper highlights the importance of recasting the underlying cause of a phenomenon.

**Keywords:** book-to-market ratio, Korea discount, risk explanation, mispricing explanation

## 9. Kim, Hyunseok — "Long-term Undervaluation of the Korean Stock Market and International Comparative Study on Long-term Changes in Stock Price Multiples"
**Source:** Financial Management Research, Vol. 43, No. 1 (2026)

**Original title:** 한국 증시의 장기적 저평가 현상과 주가배수 장기변화에 관한 국제 비교 연구

**Abstract (translated from Korean):**
This study empirically analyzed the existence and persistence of the Korea discount across 48 countries from 1990 to 2023, and further identified countries that achieved long-term increases in stock price multiples to analyze the key factors driving multiple expansion.

First, comparing Korean listed firms' stock price multiples across 48 countries, the Korean market showed persistently low levels in major relative valuation indicators including P/B and P/E ratios. Second, even after controlling for firm characteristics (size, profitability, growth) and country characteristics (GDP per capita, financial market development, geopolitical risk), Korean firms' stock price multiples remained statistically significantly undervalued. Analyzing subsample periods (1990s, 2000s, 2010s onward), Korean firms were consistently undervalued across all periods, indicating that the Korea discount is not limited to specific time periods or business cycles but has persisted for at least 30 years. Third, selecting countries that achieved significant long-term multiple expansion among 26 OECD countries, profitability improvement, appropriate liquidity reserves, and financial market environments facilitating external financing were found to play key roles in multiple expansion. In contrast, financial risk from debt financing, high levels of shareholder return policies, short-term trading activity, and external uncertainty such as geopolitical risk had negative effects on firm value improvement. In sum, the Korea discount is not a temporary phenomenon but shows persistence over at least 30 years, suggesting that resolving Korean market undervaluation requires long-term strengthening of profitability foundations, financial structure stability, and financial market development rather than short-term stimulus.

**Keywords:** Korea discount, P/B ratio, P/E ratio, value-up

## 10. Joo, Byung-chul — "Asymmetric Discipline by Foreign Investors and Corporate Value-Up: Focusing on Resolving Growth Disparity of Korea Discount Firms"
**Source:** Management and Information Research, Vol. 45, No. 1 (2026)

**Original title:** 외국인 투자자의 비대칭적 규율과 기업 밸류업 — 코리아 디스카운트 기업의 성장 불일치 해소를 중심으로

**Abstract (translated from Korean):**
This study analyzes how foreign investors discipline firms' growth disparity in the context of Korea's corporate value-up program aimed at resolving the Korea discount. Growth disparity is defined as the gap between Higgins' sustainable growth rate (SGR) and actual growth rate (AGR), and the study examines whether foreign investors serve as smart monitors who reduce this gap. Using firms listed on KOSPI and KOSDAQ from 2011 to 2024, fixed effects models and propensity score matching (PSM) were applied to control for firm heterogeneity and selection bias. While OLS regression showed a positive association between foreign ownership and growth disparity due to selection bias (foreign investors prefer firms with high growth potential), PSM analysis controlling for endogeneity revealed that foreign ownership significantly reduces growth disparity, demonstrating genuine disciplinary effects. This effect was asymmetric depending on firm circumstances: foreign capital corrects over-investment in over-growing firms and encourages profitable management in under-growing firms, thereby optimizing resource allocation. The study proves that foreign investors are not mere short-term profit seekers but function as a core mechanism of market discipline that corrects over- or under-investment, enhancing capital market efficiency. This implies that activating foreign investment can serve as a powerful means of improving corporate governance in resolving the Korea discount.

**Keywords:** foreign investors, sustainable growth rate, growth disparity, asymmetric discipline, corporate value-up

## 11. Park, Jin-jae — "Defense of Management Control through Cross-Shareholdings: Focusing on Strategy Validity and Possibility of Resolving the Korea Discount"
**Source:** Commercial Law Research, Vol. 43, No. 4 (2025)

**Original title:** 상호주를 통한 경영권 방어 — 전략의 유효성, 코리아 디스카운트 해소 가능성을 중심으로

**Abstract (translated from Korean; no formal abstract — summary from introduction):**
This paper examines whether the strategy of using cross-shareholdings to defend management control — as exemplified by Korea Zinc's use of the Commercial Act's cross-shareholding voting restriction during its January 2025 shareholder meeting — is legally valid and effective, and whether this strategy can have positive effects on the capital market. The paper argues that the cross-shareholding voting restriction strategy is both lawful and effective as a defense tactic. Furthermore, it contends that the existence and validity of this strategy can positively influence capital markets, as holding companies — which are central to forming the Korea discount through their low market capitalizations relative to affiliates — face higher risk of boardroom coups from listed affiliates using this strategy. This risk can induce revaluation (upward) of holding company stock prices, thereby potentially contributing to resolving the Korea discount.

**Keywords:** cross-shareholdings, management control defense, Korea discount, voting rights restriction, Pac-Man defense

## 12. Yang, Cheol-Won et al. — "What Really Explains the Korea Discount and PBR? A Comparison of the Growth Potential, Shareholder Payout, and Governance Hypotheses"
**Source:** Korean Securities Association Journal, Vol. 54, No. 5 (2025)

**Abstract (original English):**
This study investigates which factors — growth potential, shareholder payout, or corporate governance — explain the persistently low price-to-book ratios (PBRs) of Korean firms. We find that corporate governance has no significant relationship with PBR, and firms with higher shareholder payout tendencies tend to have lower PBRs. This contradicts prevailing hypotheses that associate shareholder payout and governance improvements with higher valuations. In contrast, variables linked to corporate growth potential exhibit a strong correlation with PBR. Firms with low research and development (R&D) expenditures and investments, those with a higher proportion of tangible assets (e.g., plants and land) rather than intangible capital (e.g., patents and brand value), and mature firms beyond their growth phase tend to have lower PBRs. These findings suggest that the low PBR of Korean firms primarily stems from weak growth prospects. A low PBR has traditionally been associated with high stock returns due to the value premium effect. However, this effect, both in Korea and globally, declined sharply following the 2008 global financial crisis. Therefore, the low PBR and stock returns of Korean firms can be interpreted as a consequence of the disappearance of the value premium in an environment of diminishing corporate growth potential.

**Keywords:** PBR, corporate governance, shareholder payout, growth potential, value premium

## 13. Kang, Nara & Kim, Heeju — "Expectations of Commercial Act Amendment and Market Reactions: The Potential Mitigation of the 'Korea Discount'"
**Source:** Korea International Accounting Review, No. 127 (2026)

**Abstract (original English):**
The 2025 amendment to the Korean Commercial Act represents a major institutional reform aimed at improving corporate governance and strengthening shareholder value, which has long been identified as a key source of the Korea Discount phenomenon. This study examines whether stock market reactions to the amendment differ across firms with higher exposure to the Korea Discount, thereby indirectly inferring the potential underlying causes of the Korea Discount. Firms with high exposure to the Korea Discount are identified using a widely adopted proxy from prior literature, the book-to-market ratio (BTM). Specifically, firms in the highest tercile of BTM within each industry-year are classified as the Korea Discount risk group. Market reactions to the amendment are analyzed across two stages based on the degree of information realization and certainty. The first stage captures preliminary expectations, with the event date set as June 2, 2025 (the day before the presidential election). The second stage reflects finalized expectations, with the event date set as July 3, 2025 (when the amendment was formally passed by the National Assembly). The results show that firms in the Korea Discount risk group — those with high BTM ratios — exhibit significantly positive abnormal returns at both the preliminary and finalized event dates. Moreover, the magnitude of the differential market reaction is larger around the parliamentary passage of the amendment than around the presidential election. In contrast, no significant differential reaction is observed among firms with high controlling shareholder ownership, suggesting that market expectations regarding the amendment are not a uniform reassessment of corporate governance, but are selectively reflected in firms where the amendment is expected to have greater substantive effects. These findings imply that corporate governance reform plays an important role in policy discussions aimed at mitigating the Korea Discount.

**Keywords:** Korea Discount, corporate governance reform, Commercial Act Amendment

## 14. The Economist — "Minority Report: The Korea Discount"
**Source:** The Economist, Vol. 402 (2012)

**Abstract (adapted from article text — no formal abstract):**
It is sometimes asserted that low South Korean equity valuations stem from the threat of instability in North Korea. That explanation looked less convincing after the death of Kim Jong Il in December, when both the KOSPI 200 index and the South Korean won quickly shrugged off the news. The article argues that the prime cause of the Korea discount — wherein the KOSPI has a forward price-to-earnings ratio of under ten, below most other Asian stock markets — is poor corporate governance at the family-run chaebol conglomerates that dominate the economy. Nefarious schemes to pass on control to sons, avoid taxes, and exploit company assets for the benefit of family members are widely discussed. A 2010 CLSA survey placed South Korea third-from-bottom in Asia on governance, ahead of only Indonesia and the Philippines. The article describes "tunnelling" (awarding contracts to firms owned by family members) and "propping" (financially supporting unviable sister companies) as key mechanisms through which insiders benefit at the expense of smaller investors. If insiders can misuse shareholders' funds at will, prospective investors will reduce their expectations of future cash flows and thus attach lower valuations to stocks.

**Keywords:** corporate governance, business conditions, stockholders, price-earnings ratio, securities markets, scandals
